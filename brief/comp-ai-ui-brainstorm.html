Comp AI: UI brainstorming

Everything from the project so far that can feed the web UI: the chosen moodboard, the two brand directions sent on 14.10, UI bits already sketched in the brand file, the client's benchmarks, the guardrails and the section ideas from the homepage structure. Each item has a short comment on what to take for the website. Brand is not final (target 16.10), so treat this as a pool of ideas, not decisions. Compiled 14.10.2026.

Chosen direction: C.2 "Built to improve"

Moodboard round 2 · picked 09.10

C.2 · Brand Design file, Moodboards 1.1

"Vivid greens meet a warm red-orange in bold, directional gradients that give the brand energy and momentum. Clean typography and structured interfaces hold that energy together in one coherent system."

BoldDynamicProgressiveVibrantEnergeticAliveConfidentUnexpectedRebelliousImpactfulIntentional

UI-ready elements on the board

  • Green → orange gradient "capsule" behind a wordmark
  • "Ask Comp AI…" prompt pill with a gradient rim
  • Guided question card: "What would you like to improve in your business?"
  • Wireframe orbit globe on off-white
  • Stacked word list in a clean grotesk (Compliance / Audit / Governance…)
  • Soft 3D star sign with glow, foam-like 3D shapes

TakeOff-white base, gradients as rare, directional accents (hero, CTA band), structured cards on top. The prompt pill and question card are the most "web-native" pieces here.

C.1 "Built to adapt" · not chosen

The client asked to bring some of C.1's sophistication and dark executions into C.2.

TakeUse C.1 as the reference for the dark sections: deep green tonal range, spotlight glow, product cards with green data. Not for the light base.

02

Brand proposal: A "Wildcard" vs B "Undercurrent"

Sent 14.10 · client feedback pending

Olga's stage II proposal shows C.2 in two directions. Both already include website-like mockups, so most of what's below works whichever one wins. Loom walkthrough

A

Wildcard: "Breaking the rules, never the standards"

Bold color, confident type, a visual language that stops the scroll. Unexpected on the surface, uncompromising underneath.

PP Formula (Semi-Condensed for logotype, Light for text) · neutral, green, coral

B

Undercurrent: "Always on, never in the way"

Calm, assured presence: rich colors with a softer touch, precise type, small moments of wit. Calm on the surface, always working underneath.

GT Planar (Regular logotype, Light text) · neutral, green, coral, blue, pink

TakeA is closer to "pick a theme and go hard" (Mariano). Condensed headlines save width in nav and section titles. Fewer colors make the light/dark split simpler.

TakeB's wider palette fits the ask for secondary/tertiary colors; blue and pink could code frameworks or statuses. It's softer, so watch that it doesn't read as "too soft" (round 1 feedback on A).

OpenWhich direction, which typeface (licences: Pangram Pangram / Grilli Type), and whether the serif question is closed. Don't commit the layouts to one font before 16.10.

06

Directions for the web UI

From C.2 feedback (09.10) and the workshop

Light and dark: two voices of one brand

Light is human-facing (support, warmth). Dark is AI, agents and developers (technical, sharper). An idea, not a rule.

TakeLight page base; dark for Verifiable, agents and the final CTA. Never a fully dark site.

Use

Personality with restraint

"Not maximalism, intentional expression." Minimal, strong base; expression only where it adds value, with clear rules.

TakeWrite the rules into the section library: max one gradient moment per viewport, one accent color per section.

Use

Symbol system as product UI

A family of signs recognisable without the logo, also used as trust badges and framework statuses.

TakeBiggest opportunity on the web: icons, statuses (Live / In beta / Coming soon), Trust Center, framework chips.

Use

Depth and dimension

Today's graphics are flat; the client wants dimension (soft 3D, glass, foam).

TakeSpend it on a few hero objects (Nervy's 3D) and UI cards with real elevation, not on everything.

UseOpenwhat Nervy delivers and when

Typographic hierarchy that isn't interchangeable

Clear H1/H2/H3/eyebrow, custom details. Must not look like "well-executed contemporary SaaS".

TakeMono or uppercase eyebrows (as in the step tags), inline chips in headlines, large light display sizes.

Use

Show, don't claim

"Instead of selling AI enthusiasm, we sell what AI makes possible." Magic = time-to-value.

TakeReal product moments, numbers and before/after over illustrations of "AI". Trust should be felt, not written in the hero.

Use

Recognition test

"Remove the logo, render it in black and white: would someone still know it's Comp?"

TakeRun this on every hi-fi section before client review. Shape, type and symbols must carry it, not color alone.

Use

Minimalism and space = premium

The client reads space as premium. Logos must "hold up on a NASCAR logo wall".

TakeGenerous vertical rhythm; dense content (footer, framework list) in tight, tidy grids.

Use

07

Guardrails: what to avoid

Workshop · moodboard feedback

Avoid

Green text on green

Their current site's problem. Also any unreadable text on gradients.

Avoid

Generic GRC / cyber

Shields, locks, checkmarks, neon gradients, glowing grids, generic AI graphics.

Avoid

"Another black AI site"

All-black, identical AI pages: Profound, Vercel and Linear as a look. Dark is not the default background.

Avoid

Playful startup identity

Notion and Figma read as too playful; round 1 B was "too playful/organic", A "too soft".

Avoid

Flat green, serif-as-legacy

Claudio's dislikes. Serif is still contested (Lewis wants it).

Avoid

Competitor look-alikes

Drata (boring), Secureframe (undifferentiated). Delve, Oneleet, Drata and Anthropic all sit in orange/coral, so coral can't carry recognition alone.
