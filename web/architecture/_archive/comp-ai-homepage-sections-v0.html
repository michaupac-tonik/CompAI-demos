Comp AI: Homepage sections (v0)

The order of sections on the new homepage, before any copy. Each block says what the section is about, why it's on the page, and what we need to agree with the client. The page is a sales page for the 20-minute demo; every section either explains the product, proves it or moves the visitor towards that call. It keeps the messages today's site carries: compliance, security, integrations, support and growth.

01

Nav

Global · today: Product · Frameworks · Customers · Pricing

About

Global navigation shared by the new homepage and the ~100 existing pages that inherit it on 13.11.

Why it matters

It's the entry point to every page on the site, and most organic traffic lands on resource pages (Hub, guides, templates, comparisons) that today are only reachable from the footer.

To discuss

  • Add Resources as a new entry point for the organic-traffic pages.
  • Confirm the official product module names (site and onboarding brief differ).
  • CTA hierarchy: is Book a demo the one primary action, and what does Get started do?
  • Log in: how do returning customers reach the app today, and does it belong in the nav?

02

Hero

Exists today

About

What Comp AI is, who it's for and the one primary action, above the fold.

Why it matters

It decides in seconds whether a founder or security lead keeps reading, and it carries the main conversion: the demo call.

To discuss

  • Which narrative route leads (I, II or III from the comms framework)?
  • Which proof can sit next to the headline (rating, customer count, Series A)?
  • Audit included needs to be made precise: the homepage meta description says 'audit and pentest included', while /pricing says it depends on scope.
  • Lead angle: today's hero sells closing deals. Do we keep that, or lead with something else, such as verifiability (our suggestion, after the Delve case)?

08

Security

Exists today ('Compliance that actually improves your security') · expanded

About

How Comp AI makes a company more secure, not just certified: continuous evidence, device agents, pentests, cloud monitoring, tests you write yourself, and open source you can verify.

Why it matters

The client wants security and compliance to carry equal weight, and continuous security is where the company is heading. After the Delve case, 'fast' alone reads as a risk, so verifiability (open source, real evidence) belongs here as one part of the security story. It's also the section that speaks to developers and AI agents.

To discuss

  • How much weight does security get next to compliance?
  • Which security claims are live today, and which are the continuous-security vision?
  • Can customers bring their own auditor?
  • Name competitors or not?
