What they do (plain language)

Before a startup can sell to a bigger company, the buyer usually asks for proof that its systems are secure. That proof is a compliance certification: an independent auditor checks the company against a published standard and signs off. The common standards are SOC 2 (the de facto US one for software companies), ISO 27001 (the international one), HIPAA (US health data) and GDPR (EU privacy). Getting there traditionally means months of writing policies, screenshotting settings as evidence, and chasing colleagues.

Comp AI automates that work. AI drafts the policies from a questionnaire. Software "agents" connect to the tools a company already uses (580+ integrations such as AWS, GitHub and Google Workspace) and collect the evidence automatically. The audit is included in the price. Customers also get a dedicated Slack channel with Comp's in-house experts, and the founders consider that support a core differentiator. In their words: "in very simple terms, we help companies become compliant and then stay secure" — Claudio, kick-off 29.09, 00:36:28.

Why buyers care now: a founder suddenly needs SOC 2 because an investor or a first big customer asked for it. "So they're usually like, 'I need compliance yesterday.'" — Katya, kick-off, 01:10:55. The incumbents (Vanta, Drata) were built before the 2022–23 AI wave, and Comp positions itself as "gen three, which is basically service automation, AI first" — Claudio, kick-off, 00:57:25.

Where they're heading: they want to move from "compliance made easy" to software that does the work for you, and in the longer run to continuous security, because trust shouldn't be a once-a-year audit. Teresa asks that the site clearly separate "what Comp does today, what we're actively building, and our longer-term vision" — Teresa, comms feedback canvas, 08.10. That matters when you write claims about agents.

What the product is, and what stage it's at

It is software with a real interface, plus a human service. There's a web app at app.trycomp.ai (dashboards, policies, evidence, vendor and risk tracking, a Trust Center page customers show to their buyers) and an open-source "device agent" that runs on employees' laptops. Alongside the software, real people help each customer in Slack. Consequence: you can show the real UI. Katya shared a demo video and app access — Katya, #p_comp-ai, 29.09, and the human support is a showable asset too.

Stage: live, paying, well past launch. Founded Jan 2025, with ~1,000 customers and 25–30 staff — Lewis, kick-off, 00:39:18. They raised a $34M Series A on 17 Sep 2026 (TechCrunch). Customers can be named: the current site has 11 case studies, mostly small AI startups. Much of the code is open source on GitHub (AGPLv3), which competitors can't match.

What the client asked for from C.2 (affects the web directly)

  • Green stays as the foundation. Add secondary and tertiary colours with a clear hierarchy (primary, secondary, tertiary, neutral), and make them distinct from competitors: "Delve, OneLeet, Drata, and Anthropic all occupy variations of the orange/coral/red spectrum, while Vanta and Secureframe incorporate green." — V2 canvas, 08.10
  • Light and dark as "two complementary voices". Light is human and supportive; dark is AI-native and technical. This is "an idea, not a fixed rule". — Teresa, 06.10
  • Contrast is a hard line: "Green text on green backgrounds … remains a significant concern. It's something we already struggle with in our current identity" and "text over gradients or complex backgrounds should remain highly legible." — V2 canvas
  • Type hierarchy should not be interchangeable ("so H1/H2/H3/eyebrow/wordmark aren't all interchangeable"), and the result should not read as "a well-executed contemporary SaaS identity". — V2 canvas
  • The symbol system is the biggest opportunity. Marks could double as "trust badges or indicators for framework states such as monitored, verified, or evidence collected". On the web that means product sections, status chips and the Trust Center. — V2 canvas
  • Their brief for the web: "Website and product marketing: Clear, intuitive, credible, sophisticated." and "The goal is not maximalism. It's intentional expression within a sophisticated, recognizable system." — V2 canvas

tonik's handoff checklist

The checklist comes from Web Design x SR007, Émil's proposal (12.10) and the 2024 handoff standard. Comp was sold before the newest process, so treat this as the expected bar rather than a contract. inferred

  • Auto layout throughout. The file is tidy, with no loose drafts. Components cover nav, footer and repeated cards, without dozens of variants.
  • Colour and text styles, or Variables. The audit target is about 90% auto layout and about 80% of values bound to variables.
  • Frame widths:
    • Desktop: 1440.
    • Mobile is mandatory: 320 per the 2024 spec, or the common 375/390.
    • Tablet: for complex elements.
  • Design the states: hover, focus, disabled, form errors and success. Also design the OG image, favicon and 404.
  • Comments or notes for anything a static frame can't show, plus a Supercut walkthrough section by section.
  • After handoff, revisions go in one thread per project, with a link, a recording and a priority. The PM, not you, tells the client it's done.

  1. Days 1–3: structure before skin.

    • Agree the inventory with Arek, because it becomes the Sanity schema.
  2. Make A vs B cheap to switch. Hold type, colour and radius as variables from day one. Sketch the hero in both directions, so the client's pick doesn't cost you a rebuild.
  3. One CTA logic across the site. Today "Get Started" and "Book Demo" fight each other. The business runs on the demo call, so make it primary and decide what "Get Started" means. to confirm
  4. Answer two buyers on one homepage.
    • The founder wants speed and price.
    • The CISO wants evidence, control and less magic.
    A visible fork (by stage or by role) beats a hero that tries to please both.
  5. Proof is the design material. Use real UI from app.trycomp.ai, case-study numbers, the open-source repo, the Slack-support experience, and the symbol system as status chips (monitored / verified / evidence collected). The logo wall is missing today, so ask for logo permissions now.
